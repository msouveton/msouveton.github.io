Hello and welcome to my personal website. I am currently a **PhD student in theoretical physics** and I study radiation-matter interaction in fibrous media.

My work involves determining the effective radiative properties of a virtual fibrous medium. I use on the one hand a classical radiation and large-scale geometrical optics approach, and on the other hand, the Maxwell equations formalism of electromagnetism at small scales. In parallel, I supervise mathematics tutorials and fluid mechanics practical works for engineering school students.

This website provides more details about my work and my personal projects.

## My work

The context of my work is **thermal insulation** at very high temperatures. A typical example is the use of nanofibrous materials for the protection of rocket fairings against the heat experienced during liftoff.

My research is however theoretical and takes place within the framework of a virtual material having idealized **morphological properties**: a random stack of semi-transparent cylindrical fibers. A first part of my work was to calculate the mathematical properties of such structures, notably the chord length distributions in the fibrous phase. This result was published in [Physical Review](https://doi.org/10.1103/47ng-7wzc).

I then studied the **radiative properties** of my material at large scales, that is to say in the geometrical optics limit. To simulate radiation propagation, I use a Monte Carlo ray tracing method. Transmittance and reflectance calculations revealed the non-Beerian behavior of the medium and allowed the development of an analytical radiative model. These results are currently being published for [JQSRT](https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/journal-of-quantitative-spectroscopy-and-radiative-transfer).

In a final part, I study the behavior of the medium when the fiber scale decreases and approaches the wavelength using the discrete dipole approximation (DDA) method to solve Maxwell's equations. The interest is to observe the boundary between the laws of electromagnetism and classical radiation. Can we imagine determining a classical radiative model capable of approximating the **electromagnetic results** in the sub-lambda domain?

## My passions

I have been passionate about **astronomy** since the age of eight, following a school year on the theme of space, with a telescope won at the Corsican university contest "Faîtes de la science" and a trip to the Cité de l'Espace in Toulouse. Today, my interests are very diverse: sky observation, planetology, stellar physics, cosmology, relativity, history of science and space exploration,... I also attach great importance to popularization and I was a planetarium animator in 2023. My missions: animate the dome, answer the public's questions, but also intervene in schools and middle schools.

Regarding my thesis, it is not directly linked to astrophysics. However, one of the major figures of radiation is the famous Chandrasekhar who, among other things, left his name to the critical mass of white dwarfs. Furthermore, in electromagnetism, the DDA method was developed for the study of interstellar dust and it is a research field that interests me.

As an amateur astronome, I own a **Dobson Skywatcher GoTo 300/1500** telescope. As someone who enjoys manually locating celestial objects, I switched to a motorized mount to get started with astrophotography. However, the mount is alt-azimuth and exposure times are necessarily limited by field rotation. For now, I am only using my phone, but I perform processing on Pipp and Registax. A few results are visible [here]({{ '/projects/' | relative_url }}).

In computer science, I have liked programming since my discovery of the Casio Basic language in high school. Through contests, I integrated into an online community capable of coding in C on calculators. Shortly after, I decided to learn a real programming language. Among my options, I chose **Python** which is, since then, my language of choice. A list of my codes is presented [here]({{ '/projects/' | relative_url }}). Note also my admiration for special effects and 3D modeling. After several years on Adobe After Effects, my flagship tool today is undoubtedly Blender, a true Swiss army knife and open-source pearl.
